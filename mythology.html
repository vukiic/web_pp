Spirits, legends and ancient worlds

Japanese mythology is a world where gods walk through nature and spirits live in forests, mountains and rivers. These stories are deeply connected to Shinto beliefs, where kami represent the hidden energy of the world.

Alongside the kami, folklore brings mysterious yōkai — creatures that can be playful, dangerous, or protective. These myths shaped Japanese culture, rituals, festivals and even modern media.

Famous Kami

Amaterasu

The Sun Goddess, embodies light, life, and divine order. Her presence brings warmth, harmony, and renewal to the world, illuminating both the heavens and the hearts of mortals.

Susanoo

The Storm God, a force of chaos and raw power. Fierce and unpredictable, he commands wind and thunder, embodying destruction, rebellion, and untamed strength.

Tsukuyomi

The Moon God, represents mystery, silence, and the cold beauty of the night. Calm and distant, he rules over shadows, dreams, and the passage of time.

Yōkai creatures

Kitsune

Mystical fox spirits known for their intelligence and shapeshifting abilities. Playful yet dangerous, they blur the line between illusion and reality, often acting as tricksters or guardians.

Tengu

Proud and fearsome mountain spirits, masters of martial skill and the wind. Often seen as warriors or guardians, they punish arrogance and protect sacred places.

Oni

Powerful demons symbolizing wrath, strength, and fear. With monstrous forms and immense power, they embody humanity’s darkest impulses and unstoppable rage.

Yuki-onna

The Snow Woman and a haunting spirit born from winter storms. Beautiful and deadly, she drifts through frozen landscapes, bringing silence, sorrow, and eternal cold.

Legend spotlight

Amaterasu and the cave

When Amaterasu hid herself in a cave, the world was plunged into darkness. Only when the other kami lured her out through dance, ritual, and radiant light was balance restored to the world.
